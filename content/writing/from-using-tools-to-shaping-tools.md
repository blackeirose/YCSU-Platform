I wanted something like Monday.com for managing a project.

But I didn’t actually need Monday.com.

I needed a much smaller set of things: tasks, people, status, priorities, deadlines, and a simple way to understand the project at a glance.

In the past, the normal path would have been to find an existing platform, request a license, learn how it works, and then adjust my workflow around the structure it provides.

Instead, I used AI to build a simplified version around the way I actually wanted to work.

That experience made me think about something larger.

The people who understand a workflow best are often the people doing the work every day.

A PM understands what a PM needs from a project-management tool.

An architect understands where architectural production becomes inefficient.

A designer understands which steps interrupt the design process.

Someone can be very good at software development — or even very good at AI — and still not know exactly what tool is right for another role.

Only the person living inside that workflow really knows:

“What information do I actually need?”

“What can I remove?”

“What should remain flexible?”

“What would make this easier to use every day?”

That domain knowledge has always been valuable. What has changed is that AI is making it much easier to turn that knowledge into a working tool.

You no longer necessarily have to wait for someone else to build it, search for an existing product that is “close enough,” or reshape your process around what the software allows.

For decades, better software has expanded what architects can do.

Revit gave us capabilities that AutoCAD never had.

But does using Revit mean we are now operating at the maximum of what an architect is capable of?

I don’t think so.

Every tool expands our abilities, but it also creates boundaries. Over time, we become very good at working within those boundaries.

AI augmentation may begin to change that relationship.

Instead of only asking:

“How can I use this tool better?”

we can increasingly ask:

“What tool would help me work better?”

AI can fill some of the knowledge we don’t have, reduce time spent on low-value work, and help us build around the expertise we already have.

That is why I’m increasingly interested in AI not just as automation, but as an amplifier.

The goal is not simply to work faster.

It is to expand how much of our own experience, judgment, and ideas we are actually able to put to use.
