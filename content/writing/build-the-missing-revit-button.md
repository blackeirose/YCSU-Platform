What if I could just build the missing Revit button?

“I wish Revit could just do this one simple thing.”

As an architect working in Revit, I’ve had that thought many times. Usually, I search for a plugin, try a workaround, or accept hours of repetitive work. Even with pyRevit and other add-ins, finding the right tool takes time, sometimes money, and doesn’t always solve the problem.

Then a painfully tight deadline pushed me to try a different approach: ask AI to help me build the missing function.

About 15 minutes of collaboration produced a custom Delete Sheets tool that saved me an estimated 5–6 hours of repetitive work.

The next day, I couldn’t find a tool that met my revision-cleanup needs. Another roughly 15 minutes with AI produced a Delete Revisions tool, saving nearly half a day of manual work.

What stayed with me wasn’t just the time saved. It was realizing that I had another option.

AI in architecture is not just about graphics.

I approached these experiments as an architect, not a software developer. I understood the task and what a useful result should look like. AI helped with the code; I still needed to check that the tools worked as intended.

For architects unfamiliar with AI, seeing impressive examples can be inspiring and intimidating. It’s easy to admire the results but feel that doing something similar is out of reach—and never get started.

A small tool is a more approachable place to begin. Pick a repetitive task you know well and ask AI to help build a solution. When it helps in your daily work, you gain both a practical benefit and the confidence to try again. Along the way, you learn to explain problems, refine requirements, test results, and work through what doesn’t behave as expected. That is how you start developing the skills to collaborate with AI.

Keep the scope small. Test carefully, using a copy of the model before changing or deleting project data.

We’ve always talked about “work smarter, not harder.”

For me, that starts with thinking through the workflow and avoiding unnecessary repetition. In the past, I focused on making better use of the tools available. Now, I can also choose to improve or extend those tools with AI before starting the work—an option that used to be difficult to pursue without programming skills or dedicated support.

Small pyRevit tools are a starting point, not an endpoint. Looking ahead, that experience could lead to more integrated workflows, through MCP connections or AI agents within design software. But we don’t need to understand all of that to begin.

For me, the question is shifting from “What can AI do?” to “What problem could I solve with it—and am I willing to try?”

You don’t have to start with a big idea. Sometimes, a missing button is enough.

What’s the missing button in your daily workflow?
